**Competitive map (price vs service level)**

```
                 High service / warranty
                          │
      Seli (premium) ●    │    ● Deep Solar / Solar AC (GHS 52-85k)
                          │
                          │  ★ Proposed entrant: certified mid-market
                          │    (5 kVA ≈ GHS 52.8k incl. VAT; 3 kVA ≈ GHS 36k)
  High price ─────────────┼───────────────────────────── Low price
      Supply Master       │
      packages ●          │            ● Jiji kits (GHS 25-37k, no installer warranty)
                          │
                 Low service / warranty
```

**Market gap:** Between informal kits (cheap, no accountability) and established installers (GHS 55–85k for 5 kVA), there is room for a **certified, transparent, mid-market installer** with financing partners and maintenance plans. The SME segment, where outages cost money, is not clearly served by any player with public SME packages.

**Barriers to entry:** Energy Commission licensing and certified electricians (REG-23, ENE-19); supplier credit relationships; reputation (installations are visible and referral-driven); working capital if customers are offered credit. All of these are **surmountable at US$17k**, provided customers pay deposits.

**Market gap:** Operators cite **slow turnaround and damaged garments** as rivals' failings (SVC-13). Most price lists target households. **Bulk B2B demand** (hostels, guest houses, short-let hosts) is referenced but rarely priced publicly (SVC-33, SVC-14). A neighbourhood operator with **guaranteed 48-hour turnaround, garment tagging and subscriptions** can compete without FUA's scale.

**Barriers to entry:** Low. Equipment is available locally (SVC-16, SVC-36); the main barriers are utility reliability and building a reputation for care.

**Market gap:** Hotels and restaurants report they **cannot source enough local volume** consistently (AGR-34, snippet), but they expect prices near import parity. The gap is **consistent supply of fresh dressed birds** to chop bars and caterers, sold on freshness and reliability.

**Barriers to entry:** Moderate in capital (housing plus batch working capital), **high in skills and biosecurity**. The biggest barrier is not getting in. It is the price ceiling set by imports.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bolt Send | Platform | On-demand parcel delivery; >1.2m Accra deliveries in 2026 | In-app (no published rate card) | App | Scale, convenience | ENE-51 |
| Yango Delivery | Platform | Express courier, cargo (aboboyaa to trucks), B2B prepaid accounts and integrations | In-app | App, web | B2B tools, cargo options | ENE-30, ENE-52 |
| Swoove | Delivery start-up | Delivery for small e-commerce sellers | From GHS 15; flat GHS 20 for 48-hour (2023) | Web, API | Seller focus, low price | ENE-50 |
| Ghana Post EMS | State courier | Same-day Accra/Tema and Kumasi (claimed) | n/p | Post offices | National network | snippet only |
| Informal dispatch riders | Individuals | Ad hoc delivery for sellers | Negotiated | WhatsApp | Flexibility | ENE-56 |
| *Glovo* | *Exited Ghana May 2024* | — | — | — | — | ENE-28 |

**Market gap:** Platforms serve **on-demand, one-off** deliveries. Sellers using them have **no service contract**, face fee drift and struggle with cash handling (ENE-51, ENE-53). A **scheduled, contracted B2B service with same-day MoMo remittance of COD cash** addresses the pain point the platforms leave open.

**Barriers to entry:** Low capital, rising compliance: rider licensing, union membership, courier registration (ENE-32–34). Compliance favours organised fleets over individual riders.

| Do-it-yourself | Business owners | Own WhatsApp/Instagram | Free | — | Zero cost | — |

**Market gap:** Agency packages start at GHS 1,500–2,500/month, priced for established SMEs. **Micro-sellers** need a cheaper, bundled "go digital" package: catalogue, MoMo checkout, delivery integration. The gap is real, but it may exist because micro-sellers **will not pay** for it. That is the hypothesis to test first.

**Barriers to entry:** Very low. Defensibility comes only from specialisation and results.

## 6. Summary: competitive position of a new entrant

| Business | Intensity of competition | Clearest gap | Can a US$5–20k entrant win it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar installation | Medium | Certified mid-market installer for SMEs, with financing and maintenance | **Yes** — the gap is about trust and service, not capital |
| Laundry service | Medium | Reliable neighbourhood service plus B2B bulk contracts | Yes — locally |
| Delivery fleet | Medium–high | Contracted B2B delivery with fast COD remittance | Yes, in a niche; platforms dominate the mass market |
| Digital services | High | Bundled micro-seller package | Unproven — the gap may reflect low willingness to pay |
| Broiler farm | High | Consistent fresh supply to chop bars and caterers | Only with pre-sold off-take; price is set by imports |
