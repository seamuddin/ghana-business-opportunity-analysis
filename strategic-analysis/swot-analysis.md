# SWOT Analysis

[← Back to README](../README.md) · Related: [pestle-analysis.md](pestle-analysis.md) · [competitor-analysis.md](competitor-analysis.md) · [risk-assessment.md](risk-assessment.md) · [final-recommendation.md](final-recommendation.md)

> Strengths and weaknesses are **internal to the business model** as designed. Opportunities and threats are **external**, drawn from the PESTLE and competitor evidence. Each point carries a source ID or a model reference so the SWOT is evidence, not opinion. A **TOWS matrix** for the recommended business converts the SWOT into strategy.

---

## 1. Solar PV installation (recommended — see TOWS below)

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| Large absolute margin per sale (≈ US$1,200 per 5 kVA system) → strongest base case: US$11.0k year-1 profit, ~12-month payback ([P]) | Contribution margin only ≈ 28%, so it is sensitive to price and unit cost ([break-even-analysis.md](../financial-analysis/break-even-analysis.md)) |
| Deposit-before-purchase model needs no stock financing | Depends on a certified technician (key-person risk) |
| Mid-market price below established installers (ENE-44) | No track record or references at launch |
| Recurring maintenance revenue builds with the installed base | Highest conservative-case loss (−US$14.7k) |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|
| Outages 2024–2026 and quarterly tariff resets (ENE-03–12) | Cedi depreciation: −US$16.2k year-1 impact in the FX stress test ([P]) |
| Only 7.6 MW small residential PV installed; 214 MW distributed targeted by 2030 (ENE-13) | Price pressure from marketplace kits (ENE-18) |
| Bank solar loans (ENE-46, ENE-47) and a US$200m rooftop programme (ENE-14) | Buyers may **delay purchases** until the proposed duty/VAT exemption becomes law (REG-18) |
| Proposed 2027 duty/VAT exemption could lower system prices and widen demand (REG-18) | Grid improvements (2,500+ transformer replacements, ENE-06) could weaken backup demand over time |

### TOWS matrix — solar installation

| | **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunities** | **SO — grow:** Use the deposit model plus bank-loan partnerships (Ecobank, Stanbic) to sell to outage-hit SMEs; publish transparent package prices to win buyers moving up from kits. | **WO — build credibility:** Install a live demonstration system; seek manufacturer certification; apply for programme installer lists under the rooftop programme (ENE-14). |
| **Threats** | **ST — protect margin:** Quote in cedis indexed to USD with 7-day validity; buy equipment only after the deposit; sell maintenance plans so revenue does not depend only on new systems. | **WT — limit downside:** Stage the investment (validation first); hold a reserve equal to the conservative-case gap (~US$5.8k); set stop-loss rules ([final-recommendation.md §6](final-recommendation.md#6-stage-gates-and-stop-loss-rules)). |

## 2. Laundry service

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| High contribution margin (≈ 76%) | Small revenue base; fixed costs (labour, rent) dominate |
| Local costs and revenue → naturally hedged against FX (FX test −US$1.2k) | Gross margin depends on high machine utilisation |
| Simple operations, trainable staff | Demand evidence is mostly operators' claims |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|
| Urbanisation and time-poor professionals (MAC-14) | Utility outages and tariff increases (ENE-05, ENE-09) |
| Under-served B2B bulk demand: hostels, guest houses (SVC-33) | Well-funded entrants with 24-hour service and free pickup (SVC-13, SVC-34) |
| Subscriptions create recurring revenue | Informal washers at much lower prices |

## 3. Broiler farm

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| Strongest demand volume evidence (82% of chicken imported, AGR-03) | Low contribution margin (≈ 22%); a 20% price cut erases the year-1 profit more than three times over ([P]) |
| VAT-exempt local poultry and feed (REG-19) | Working-capital heavy (each batch ≈ GHS 66–71k, AGR-33) |
| Manure by-product revenue | Husbandry and biosecurity skills required |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|
| Government self-sufficiency targets; school local-procurement directive (AGR-02, AGR-38) | Import price ceiling (GHS 33–40/kg vs local ≈ GHS 49/kg; AGR-12, AGR-33) |
| Processing off-take pilots (AGR-37) | Subsidised programme flocks and a 2026 glut (AGR-05, AGR-06, AGR-36) |
| Festive price peaks (sources expected Christmas 2026 prices to be ≥ 15% higher; snippet) | Avian influenza in the region (AGR-07, AGR-08); feed price spikes (AGR-13) |

## 4. Delivery fleet

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| Low unit cost per drop, scalable bike by bike | Low price per drop (GHS 25) → needs route density |
| Contracted B2B model with COD remittance addresses a known pain point (ENE-53) | Rider management and cash control are hard |
| Naturally hedged (FX test −US$1.2k) | Thin margin of safety (≈ 26%) |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|
| Social commerce growth; Bolt non-restaurant orders +154% (ENE-29, ENE-54) | Platform price competition (ENE-50–52) |
| Okada legalisation and union rules favour organised fleets (ENE-31–33) | Fuel subsidy ending; accidents and theft |
| Expansion to Kumasi (Yango entered Feb 2026, ENE-30) | Regulation still bedding in (L.I. 2519) |

## 5. Digital services

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| Lowest capital (≈ US$8.5k) and lowest risk-register score | No evidence of willingness to pay among micro-firms |
| High contribution margin (≈ 78%) | Loss-making in year 1 in the base case ([P]) |
| Eligible for foreign investors at low total cost | People-bound; key-person risk |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|
| Only ~37% of firms use digital payments (ENE-40); WhatsApp near-universal (ENE-23) | Very low barriers to entry; freelancers and DIY tools |
| Bundling with delivery and payments | Platform changes (algorithms, ad costs, fees) |
| Training workshops as lead generation | Agencies already priced from GHS 1,500/month (ENE-57) |

## Cross-SWOT insight

The SWOTs reveal a consistent trade-off. **The businesses with the strongest demand (solar, broilers) carry the most external cost risk** (FX, feed, import prices). **The businesses with the most controllable costs (laundry, digital) have the weakest demand evidence.** Solar is recommended because its main threats (FX and pricing) can be **managed operationally**: deposits, USD-indexed quotes and maintenance revenue. The broiler farm's main threat, the import price ceiling, cannot be managed by a small producer.
