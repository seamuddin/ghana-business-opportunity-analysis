# Stage 1 — Opportunity Identification (Long List)

[← Back to README](../README.md) · Next: [opportunity-comparison.md](opportunity-comparison.md) → [shortlisted-businesses.md](shortlisted-businesses.md)

> **Purpose:** Identify a broad, evidence-led long list across ten sector groups before any filtering. Fifteen opportunities were profiled, and two more (event-equipment rental, car washing) were considered but dropped at the evidence gate because no credible demand or pricing data could be found.
>
> Evidence labels: **[F]** verified fact · **[E]** estimate · **[A]** assumption. Investment ranges are **[E]** first-pass estimates built from the price evidence in [data/sources.md](../data/sources.md). They are refined only for the five shortlisted businesses.

## O01 — Solar PV installation & maintenance (3–5 kVA hybrid systems)

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | Design, supply and install grid-tied hybrid solar systems (panels + inverter + lithium battery) for homes, shops, clinics and offices; sell annual maintenance plans. |
| Market demand | **[F]** Load-shedding or outages in 2024, 2025 and 2026, attributed to overloaded transformers, weak cables and the 2026 Akosombo fire (ENE-03–06). **[F]** Electricity tariffs reset quarterly: +9.86% Jan 2026, +3.49% Jul 2026 (ENE-09, ENE-11). **[F]** Only 7.6 MW of small residential PV and 86 MW of distributed PV were installed by end-2025, against a 2030 target of 214 MW distributed (ENE-13). **[F]** A US$200m rooftop programme targets ~4,000 systems (ENE-14). |
| Target customers | Middle-income homeowners; SMEs with refrigeration or computers (pharmacies, salons, cold drinks retailers, printing shops); small clinics and schools. |
| Customer problem | Outages destroy stock and stop trading; petrol generators are noisy and expensive to run; tariffs keep rising. |
| Existing alternatives | Petrol/diesel generators; inverter + battery backup without panels; doing nothing. |
| Competitor landscape | Fragmented: online equipment retailers (Supply Master), informal sellers on Jiji, installers (Tino Solutions and others), PAYG solar-home-system providers at the low end (Bboxx), C&I developers at the top end (Daystar, REDAVIA). See [competitor-analysis.md](../strategic-analysis/competitor-analysis.md). |
| Startup requirements | Energy Commission electrical-wiring certification and licensing for the lead technician (REG-23); tools and safety equipment; a demonstration system; supplier credit lines; a small showroom/office. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$12,000–18,000** |
| Revenue model | Project margin on installed systems (customer pays a deposit up front), plus annual maintenance contracts and battery upgrades. |
| Potential challenges | FX exposure (imported equipment); quality of cheap components; technical liability; customer financing. |
| Scalability | High — add installation crews, B2B/SME contracts, bank-financed customer programmes. |

## O02 — Last-mile delivery for online and social-commerce sellers

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | A motorbike fleet providing same-day, scheduled delivery and cash-on-delivery (COD) collection for Instagram/WhatsApp/TikTok sellers, pharmacies and small retailers in Accra. |
| Market demand | **[F]** 28.8m internet users (80.4%) in Oct 2026 (ENE-23). **[F]** Bolt Food non-restaurant orders +154% year on year in H1 2026 (ENE-29). **[F]** Jumia reports strong quarters in Ghana in 2026 (ENE-24). **[F]** Commercial motorcycles legalised, with rider licensing and union membership required (ENE-31–33). |
| Target customers | Online micro-sellers (fashion, cosmetics, food, electronics), pharmacies, bakeries, small B2B distributors. |
| Customer problem | Unreliable informal riders, lost COD cash, no tracking, no proof of delivery. |
| Existing alternatives | Informal "dispatch riders", platform couriers (Yango, Bolt), courier firms (Swoove and others), sellers delivering themselves. |
| Competitor landscape | Platforms serve consumer demand; Glovo exited Ghana in May 2024 (ENE-28); informal riders are numerous. |
| Startup requirements | 4–6 motorbikes, rider licences and courier registration (GHS 570 each, ENE-34), insurance, phones, a dispatch/tracking app, union/company membership. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$10,000–16,000** |
| Revenue model | Per-drop fees (Accra average ≈ GHS 45, ENE-35), monthly B2B contracts, a COD handling fee. |
| Potential challenges | Rider churn and theft risk; fuel prices; accidents; price competition from platforms. |
| Scalability | High — add bikes per contract; expand to Kumasi and Takoradi. |

## O03 — Broiler production with direct off-take

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | A staggered-batch broiler farm (~1,000 birds sold per month) with on-farm or contract dressing, selling to chop bars, restaurants, caterers and festive buyers. |
| Market demand | **[F]** Poultry meat imports US$180m in 2024 (mirror data, AGR-01). The minister cites US$400m (AGR-02); one estimate puts imports at ~82% of supply (AGR-03). **[F]** Government targets 25% self-sufficiency in 2026 (AGR-02). |
| Target customers | Chop bars and restaurants; caterers; households at Christmas/Easter/Eid; supermarkets for dressed birds. |
| Customer problem | Preference for fresh local chicken, but supply is inconsistent; imported frozen chicken is cheaper but perceived as lower quality. |
| Existing alternatives | Imported frozen chicken (≈ GHS 33.40/kg, AGR-12); live-bird markets; backyard flocks; Nkoko Nkitinkiti beneficiaries. |
| Competitor landscape | Integrated farms (Akate, Darko, Topman), thousands of small farms, and import distributors. |
| Startup requirements | Poultry house(s), brooders, feeders and drinkers, a water supply, a generator or solar backup, veterinary support, feed supplier accounts. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$12,000–18,000** |
| Revenue model | Sale of live and dressed birds; manure as a by-product. |
| Potential challenges | **[F]** Feed is ~70% of cost (AGR-13); avian influenza (AGR-07); price ceiling set by frozen imports; off-take problems reported in 2026 (AGR-06). |
| Scalability | Medium — scale is capped by housing, capital and biosecurity; growth through contract growing or processing. |

## O04 — Laundry & dry-cleaning with pickup and delivery

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | A neighbourhood processing shop in an Accra middle-income area offering wash & fold, ironing and dry cleaning, with scheduled pickup and delivery and monthly plans. |
| Market demand | **[F]** Urban share of population 56.7% at the 2021 census (MAC-14), ~59% by 2023 (SVC-22). **[F]** New entrants are scaling: a 24-hour facility with free pickup opened in East Legon in Oct 2026 (SVC-13). Demand evidence is otherwise anecdotal (operator claims). |
| Target customers | Working professionals, young families, students in private hostels, Airbnb hosts, small hotels and guest houses. |
| Customer problem | Time poverty; water and power interruptions at home; damaged garments and slow turnaround from informal washers (SVC-13). |
| Existing alternatives | Washing at home, house helps, informal washermen, self-service laundries, app-based operators. |
| Competitor landscape | FUA Xpress, Laundry Chief, Wash N Go and others, plus a large informal segment. |
| Startup requirements | 2–3 semi-commercial washers, dryers, pressing equipment, a generator/backup, a small shop, a delivery motorbike, an AMA business operating permit. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$10,000–16,000** |
| Revenue model | Per-item and per-kg pricing; monthly subscriptions; B2B contracts (hostels, guest houses). |
| Potential challenges | Electricity and water costs; outages; garment-damage liability; customer acquisition. |
| Scalability | Medium–high — hub-and-spoke model (collection points feeding one processing hub). |

## O05 — SME digital-commerce enablement services

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | A small agency that sets up and runs digital sales for micro and small firms: WhatsApp Business catalogues, payment links and MoMo merchant accounts, Google Business profiles, simple web stores, social media content and ads, on monthly retainers. |
| Market demand | **[F]** Only ~37% of enterprises use digital payments (ENE-40), while there are 26.4m active mobile money accounts (MAC-04) and 28.8m internet users (ENE-23). **[F]** 1.87m establishments, 90.4% micro (MAC-20). Evidence of **paid** demand from micro firms was not found. |
| Target customers | Small restaurants, boutiques, pharmacies, beauty and wellness businesses, schools, real-estate agents. |
| Customer problem | No time or skills to sell online; weak visibility; cash-only operations. |
| Existing alternatives | Do-it-yourself on Facebook/Instagram, freelancers, nephews and friends, larger agencies. |
| Competitor landscape | Very fragmented and price-competitive; low barriers to entry. |
| Startup requirements | Laptops, cameras/phones, software subscriptions, a small office or co-working desk, a portfolio of pilot clients. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$5,000–8,000** |
| Revenue model | Set-up fees plus monthly retainers; commission on ad spend; training workshops. |
| Potential challenges | Client churn; low willingness to pay; collecting payment from clients. |
| Scalability | Medium — limited by skilled staff; productised packages help. |

## O06 — Tilapia cold-chain distribution (first reserve)

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | Buy fresh tilapia from Volta/Asutsuare farms, keep it in the cold chain, and supply retailers, chop bars and restaurants in Accra and Tema. |
| Market demand | **[F]** Domestic fish output meets 43.6% of national need (AGR-19). **[F]** Aquaculture reached 121,809 t in 2024, with a 2026 target of 178,899 t (AGR-18). **[F]** Tilapia sells at GHS 35–38/kg farm-gate and roughly three times that at retail (AGR-19). |
| Target customers | Fish retailers, chop bars ("tilapia joints"), restaurants, hotels. |
| Customer problem | Inconsistent freshness and supply; spoilage. |
| Existing alternatives | Market traders, farm direct sales, frozen imported fish. |
| Competitor landscape | Large farms run their own distribution (e.g., Tropo Farms, AGR-28); many traders. |
| Startup requirements | Chest freezers or a small cold room, a tricycle or van hire, ice, an FDA cold-store licence. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$10,000–18,000** |
| Revenue model | Trading margin per kg; delivery fees. |
| Potential challenges | **[F]** FDA closed non-compliant cold-storage facilities in 2026 (SVC-21); spoilage; farm supply contracts. For foreign investors this is a trading activity (US$500k under Act 1173). |
| Scalability | Medium. |

## O07 — Maize aggregation, drying & storage for feed

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | Buy maize at harvest in producing districts, dry and store it properly (hermetic bags), and sell it to feed millers and poultry farms in the lean season. |
| Market demand | **[F]** Maize production 4.6 Mt vs demand 3.6 Mt in 2025 (AGR-22). **[F]** Feed is ~70% of poultry cost and maize ~60% of feed (AGR-13). |
| Target customers | Feed mills, medium poultry farms, NAFCO. |
| Customer problem | Aflatoxin and moisture problems; unreliable supply. |
| Existing alternatives | Traders, NAFCO, mills buying direct. |
| Competitor landscape | Many aggregators; NAFCO as buyer of last resort. |
| Startup requirements | Warehouse, moisture meter, tarpaulins/dryer, hermetic bags, stock capital. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$15,000–30,000** (stock-heavy). |
| Revenue model | Seasonal price spread. **[F]** Market GHS 240–250/100 kg in Jan 2026 vs a GHS 450 NAFCO floor (AGR-15, AGR-16). |
| Potential challenges | The national surplus compresses the seasonal spread; storage losses; government grain releases. |
| Scalability | Medium. |

## O08 — Tomato aggregation with solar cold storage

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | A solar-powered cold room at a market or farm cluster, charging per crate stored, plus aggregation for buyers. |
| Market demand | **[F]** 30–45% tomato post-harvest loss (AGR-23); processed tomato imports ~US$150m/yr (AGR-24). |
| Target customers | Tomato traders ("market queens"), farmers' groups, processors. |
| Customer problem | Spoilage within days of harvest; distress selling. |
| Existing alternatives | Selling immediately at low prices; imported paste. |
| Competitor landscape | Few cold-storage-as-a-service operators were identified. |
| Startup requirements | A solar cold room (the dominant cost), site lease, crates. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$18,000–35,000** — at or above the budget ceiling. |
| Revenue model | Storage fee per crate per day; aggregation margin. |
| Potential challenges | Willingness to pay for storage is unproven in Ghana; high capex share; seasonality. |
| Scalability | Medium. |

## O09 — Branded gari processing & packaging

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | Small processing unit producing hygienic, branded and fortified gari for supermarkets and export to the diaspora. |
| Market demand | Staple food. **[F]** NAFCO floor price GHS 10/kg (AGR-15). Gari export data was not found. |
| Target customers | Supermarkets (17% of food retail, AGR-01), diaspora shops, schools. |
| Customer problem | Sand and grit in market gari; inconsistent quality. |
| Existing alternatives | Unbranded market gari; established brands (e.g., Kivo Gari Mix). |
| Competitor landscape | Many processors; branded incumbents. |
| Startup requirements | Grater, press, roaster, packaging, FDA product registration (REG-22). |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$8,000–15,000** |
| Revenue model | Wholesale and retail packaged sales. |
| Potential challenges | Commodity pricing; low margins; FDA compliance. |
| Scalability | Medium. |

## O10 — LPG cylinder-exchange point under the Cylinder Recirculation Model

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | A licensed cylinder-exchange/distribution point for a bottling company under the NPA's Cylinder Recirculation Model (CRM). |
| Market demand | **[F]** LPG penetration ~43% vs a 50% target for 2030 (ENE-37); the CRM roll-out continued in 2026 (ENE-38, ENE-39). |
| Target customers | Urban and peri-urban households, chop bars. |
| Customer problem | Unsafe refilling; distance to refill stations. |
| Existing alternatives | Refill stations (being phased out), charcoal. |
| Competitor landscape | Bottlers and their appointed distributors control the channel. |
| Startup requirements | NPA-compliant premises, cylinder stock/deposits, safety equipment, a distributor appointment. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$15,000–40,000** |
| Revenue model | Regulated distribution margin per cylinder. |
| Potential challenges | Regulatory uncertainty during the roll-out; margins set by the regulator; safety liability. |
| Scalability | Medium. |

## O12 — Frozen fish & chicken cold-store retail

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | A neighbourhood "cold store" retailing imported frozen mackerel, chicken and sausages. |
| Market demand | Large and steady. **[F]** Fish imports US$216m (2023, AGR-20); poultry imports US$180m (2024, AGR-01). |
| Target customers | Households, chop bars. |
| Customer problem | Access to affordable protein. |
| Existing alternatives | Thousands of existing cold stores and market traders. |
| Competitor landscape | Saturated. **[F]** FDA closed 15 Greater Accra cold stores in July 2026 (SVC-21). |
| Startup requirements | Chest freezers (≈ GHS 4,899–5,999 each, SVC-26), generator, stock, FDA licence. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$6,000–12,000** |
| Revenue model | Retail margin. |
| Potential challenges | Thin margins; power outages; FDA compliance. Market and petty trading is reserved for Ghanaians (SVC-30). |
| Scalability | Low. |

## O13 — Plastic waste aggregation for recyclers

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | Buy sorted PET/HDPE from pickers, bale it and sell to recyclers. |
| Market demand | **[F]** ~0.84 Mt of plastic waste per year, ~9.5% collected for recycling (SVC-17). |
| Target customers | Recyclers and pelletisers. |
| Customer problem | Recyclers need clean, sorted feedstock. |
| Existing alternatives | Informal aggregators; Coliba-type buy-back schemes. |
| Competitor landscape | Contracting. **[F]** Kumasi bale prices fell from GHS 140 to GHS 40 in 2026 and several balers closed (SVC-18). |
| Startup requirements | Baler, scale, yard, tricycle. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$5,000–12,000** |
| Revenue model | Per-kg spread. |
| Potential challenges | Price collapse; EPR law still in draft (SVC-17); EU export restrictions from Nov 2026. |
| Scalability | Low under current prices. |

## O14 — Standalone mobile money agency

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | Cash-in/cash-out kiosk for MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash and AT Money. |
| Market demand | **[F]** GHS 4.5tn transaction value in 2025 (ENE-26). |
| Target customers | Unbanked and underbanked users. |
| Customer problem | Access to cash. |
| Existing alternatives | ~491,000 active agents (ENE-25). |
| Competitor landscape | Saturated. **[F]** Only 46% of registered agents were active in Jun 2025 (SVC-24). |
| Startup requirements | Float capital, kiosk, telco agent contract. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$2,000–5,000** |
| Revenue model | Commission (~0.4–1% of cash-out value; snippet-level evidence). |
| Potential challenges | Fraud, robbery, thin commissions. |
| Scalability | Low — viable only as an add-on to another shop. |

## O15 — Sachet water production

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Concept | Purify and bag 500 ml sachet water for wholesale distribution. |
| Market demand | Very large daily consumption. |
| Target customers | Retailers, hawkers, events. |
| Customer problem | Affordable drinking water. |
| Existing alternatives | 1,200+ registered brands and many unregistered producers (SVC-20/21, snippet-level counts). |
| Competitor landscape | Saturated. **[F]** The producers' association sets recommended prices: GHS 8 ex-factory per bag (SVC-20). |
| Startup requirements | Borehole/treatment, sachet machine, UV sterilisation and microbial testing, FDA licence, a truck. |
| Initial investment | **[E] US$25,000–60,000** — fails the budget gate. |
| Revenue model | Per-bag sales. |
| Potential challenges | **Reserved for Ghanaians** under Act 1173 (SVC-30); price controls; FDA enforcement. |
| Scalability | Medium for incumbents. |
