## 2. Why Ghana, why now

1. **Post-crisis stabilisation.** Ghana completed its IMF Extended Credit Facility in July 2026 and moved to a non-lending Policy Coordination Instrument (MAC-10). Two rating agencies upgraded the sovereign in 2026 (MAC-11, MAC-12).
2. **Falling cost of money.** Average lending rates fell from 24.15% to 15.94% in a year (MAC-04).
3. **A large, young, digitally connected population**, with persistent youth unemployment (21.9%, MAC-13). This means both labour availability and a strong policy push for job-creating enterprises.
4. **Visible infrastructure gaps** — power reliability, logistics and cold chain — that small service businesses can fill profitably (see [industry-analysis.md](industry-analysis.md)).

## 4. Rules for foreign investors

> **This changed in July 2026 and is the most important finding for non-Ghanaian readers.** The Ghana Investment Promotion Centre Act 2013 (Act 865), with its US$200k / US$500k / US$1m minimum capital rules, has been **repealed**. It was replaced by the **Ghana Investment Promotion Authority Act, 2026 (Act 1173)**, assented on 15 July 2026 (REG-01–REG-05).

| Rule under Act 1173 | Detail | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum capital — non-trading businesses | **No blanket minimum** for wholly foreign or joint-venture non-trading enterprises (sector laws may still apply) | [F] | REG-04, REG-05 |
| Minimum capital — trading | **US$500,000 cash equity** plus at least **75% skilled Ghanaian staff**. Also applies where a non-citizen is a beneficial owner or director of a Ghanaian-registered trading company (s.35(3)) | [F] | REG-05, REG-06 |
| Activities reserved for Ghanaians (s.32) | (1) market sales, petty trading, hawking, stall selling; (2) beauty salons and barbershops; (3) taxi/car hire with a fleet under 25 vehicles; (4) exercise books and basic stationery; (5) retail of finished pharmaceuticals; (6) production, supply or retail of sachet water | [F] | REG-04, REG-05 |
| Registration | Register with ORC, then with GIPA before operating; GIPA decides within 5 days; **renewed annually** | [F] | REG-05 |
| GIPA registration fee (fee schedule of Feb 2026) | Wholly foreign **US$5,250**; joint venture **US$3,500**; foreign/JV trading US$7,000 | [F] | REG-08 |
| Expatriate quota | 2 permits for capital of US$50k–500k, rising with capital. **No automatic quota below US$50k** | [F] | REG-11 |
| Fronting | Using a Ghanaian nominee to hide foreign ownership is prohibited and penalised | [F] | REG-06 |

### What this means at a US$5,000–20,000 budget

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can a foreigner own 100% of a small non-trading business? | **Yes**, since July 2026 — but the GIPA fee alone (US$5,250 wholly foreign; US$3,500 JV) equals **18–105% of the budget** (US$3,500 on US$20,000 up to US$5,250 on US$5,000). A renewal fee recurs every year (US$700–1,400 under the Feb 2026 schedule, REG-08). |
| Can a foreigner run a shop, cold store or import-resale business? | **Effectively no.** These are trading activities (US$500k) or reserved activities. |
| Can a foreign founder get a work permit through the business? | **Not through the automatic quota** below US$50k of capital. A permit would have to be pursued separately with the Ghana Immigration Service, and its fees are reported inconsistently (REG-12). |
| Most practical structure | A **Ghanaian-led business with a foreign financial partner** in a **non-trading** activity, registered as a JV, with transparent beneficial ownership and legal advice on the trading definition. |

**Implication for this study:** The financial models are built for a **Ghanaian (or resident) owner-operator**. For each shortlisted business, [shortlisted-businesses.md](../opportunities/shortlisted-businesses.md) adds a foreign-investor note covering eligibility, the extra GIPA cost and structural risks.

## 5. Taxes a small business will meet (2026)

| Tax | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Modified Taxation Scheme (individuals) | 3% of turnover for turnover above GHS 20,000, up to a ceiling raised to **GHS 750,000** by Act 1178 (2026). Professionals and multi-outlet owners are excluded. | REG-16 |
| Corporate income tax | 25% + Growth & Sustainability Levy 2.5% of profit before tax (to 2028) | REG-13 |
| VAT | 20% effective (15% + 2.5% NHIL + 2.5% GETFund); registration threshold **GHS 750,000** for goods (extended to services by Act 1177); COVID-19 levy and flat-rate scheme abolished from 1 Jan 2026 | REG-14, REG-15 |
| VAT exemptions relevant to agriculture | Animal feed and ingredients, veterinary drugs, poultry raised in Ghana, fertiliser | REG-19 |
| Import duty | ECOWAS CET bands 0/5/10/20/35% plus other levies | REG-17 |
| Solar equipment | **Proposed** exemption of solar panels and batteries from import duty and VAT in the 2027 Budget (12 Nov 2026) — **not yet law** | REG-18 |
| E-levy | Repealed from April 2025 | ENE-27 |
| SSNIT | 13% employer + 5.5% employee | REG-20 |

## 6. Operating realities that do not appear in statutes

| Reality | Evidence | Planning response in this study |
|---|---|---|
| Landlords demand rent advances well beyond the legal six months | A 2026 listing demands 5 years' advance; Rent Act enforcement from April 2026 (SVC-03, SVC-07) | 12 months' prepaid rent budgeted as base case |
| Power outages | 2024–2026 outage episodes (ENE-03–06) | Backup power budgeted for laundry and poultry; outages treated as a demand driver for solar |
| Informal competition | 92.3% of establishments informal (MAC-20) | Differentiate on reliability, receipts and digital service |
| Price-sensitive customers | Nearly 1 in 5 households spend >75% of income on food (MAC-22) | Target middle-income and SME customers first |
