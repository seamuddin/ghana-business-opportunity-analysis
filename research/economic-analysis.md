# Economic Analysis — Ghana, November 2026

[← Back to README](../README.md) · Related: [country-overview.md](country-overview.md) · [industry-analysis.md](industry-analysis.md)

> **Bottom line:** Ghana's macro environment in late 2026 is the most favourable for small-business entry since before the 2022 crisis: growth near 6%, inflation near 5–6%, and lending rates that have fallen by about 8 percentage points in a year. The two macro risks that matter most for a USD 5,000–20,000 business are **(1) renewed cedi depreciation**, which hits any business that imports its inputs, and **(2) utility and fuel cost pass-through**, as the fuel subsidy ends and electricity tariffs are reset quarterly.

Evidence labels: **[F]** verified fact · **[E]** estimate · **[A]** assumption · **[P]** projection. Source IDs resolve in [data/sources.md](../data/sources.md).

## 2. Growth: broad-based, services-led

- **[F]** The economy grew 6.0% in 2025 and 6.0% in Q2 2026. Services make up 45.9% of GDP, industry 31.3% and agriculture 22.8% (MAC-01).
- **[F]** In Q2 2026, services grew 8.0% and ICT 30.9%, while agriculture grew only 3.9% (MAC-04).
- **[F]** The IMF is more cautious, projecting 4.9% for 2026 (MAC-10).

**Implication:** Demand growth is concentrated in **urban services, digital activity and consumption**. Agriculture grows more slowly but remains the largest employer of the poor and the main source of import-substitution opportunities. Agribusiness ideas need a specific supply-gap argument, not just "agriculture is big".

## 3. Prices: low inflation, but not for transport and utilities

- **[F]** Headline inflation was 5.5% in October 2026, with food inflation at only 2.8% (MAC-09).
- **[F]** Transport inflation (14.1%) and housing/water/energy inflation (10.5%) run at two to three times the headline rate (MAC-09).
- **[F]** NPA price floors for 1–15 November 2026 were GHS 15.39/L for petrol and GHS 17.41/L for diesel; pump prices were about GHS 16.4–17.0 and GHS 18.5–19.0 (MAC-18, MAC-19). Media reports indicate the government fuel subsidy is expected to end on 30 November 2026 (snippet-level evidence only, so treated as a **risk**, not a fact).

**Implication:** For a new business, **energy and transport are the inflation-exposed cost lines**. Low food inflation helps food-processing margins on the input side but also limits how far retail food prices can rise. The models apply higher cost escalation to fuel and power in the conservative scenario.

## 4. Interest rates: cheaper, but debt is still expensive for a start-up

- **[F]** The policy rate has been held at 14.0%. Average lending rates fell from 24.15% to 15.94% in twelve months (MAC-04, MAC-05).
- **[E]** The real lending rate is about 10% (15.94% nominal minus 5.5% inflation). For an unproven micro-enterprise without collateral, the actual offer will be higher than the average.

**Implication:** All five financial models in this study are **equity-funded**: no debt is assumed. Debt becomes realistic for expansion once a business has 12 months of bank statements and mobile money records. Where suppliers or banks offer **customer financing** (for example, bank solar loans), that is used to support customers' purchasing power, not the business's own balance sheet.

## 5. The exchange rate: the single biggest macro risk

| 2024 average | 14.23 | MAC-06 |
| 2024 close | 14.70 | MAC-06 |
| 2025 average | 12.50 | MAC-06 |
| 2025 close | 10.45 | MAC-06 |
| PURC tariff assumption (Q3 2026) | 11.22 | ENE-11 |
| Interbank, 13 Nov 2026 (**model rate**) | 11.70 | MAC-07 |

- **[F]** The cedi appreciated 40.7% in 2025 (MAC-03), then gave back about 10.7% between January and mid-November 2026 (MAC-06, MAC-07).
- **[E]** Forex-bureau rates run about 5% weaker than interbank. An investor converting USD at a bureau would receive roughly 5% more cedis than the model assumes. The model deliberately uses the lower interbank rate, which is the conservative choice for USD-funded investors.

**Implication:** Businesses that **import their inputs and sell in cedis** (solar equipment, imported packaging, spare parts) carry the most currency risk. Businesses with **local inputs and local sales** (poultry using local maize, services) are naturally hedged. The sensitivity analysis includes a dedicated stress test: a 20% cedi depreciation with no ability to re-price for 12 months (see [break-even-analysis.md](../financial-analysis/break-even-analysis.md)).

## 6. Labour and wages

- **[F]** The national daily minimum wage is GHS 21.77 from 1 January 2026, up 9% (MAC-15). At 26 working days this is about GHS 566 per month, or roughly USD 48 **[E]**.
- **[E]** Advertised urban wages for semi-skilled roles are well above the minimum: shop attendants GHS 1,200–3,000, drivers GHS 1,500–2,000, dispatch riders GHS 1,000–2,000 and solar technicians GHS 2,000–3,000 per month (SVC-09 to SVC-12, listings).
- **[F]** Youth unemployment is 21.9% and about 2 million young people are not in education, employment or training (MAC-13).

**Implication:** Labour is available and affordable. The binding constraints for small businesses are **capital items** (equipment, vehicles, rent advances) and **skills** (licensed electricians, food-safety competence), not headcount. The models use mid-points of advertised wage ranges, not the minimum wage, and add SSNIT contributions.

## 7. Utility costs

- **[F]** PURC's 2026–2030 tariff order raised average electricity tariffs by 9.86% from 1 January 2026. Q2 2026 brought a 4.81% cut, Q3 2026 a 3.49% rise, and Q4 2026 no change (MAC-16, MAC-17, ENE-09 to ENE-12).
- **[E]** The non-residential energy charge from 1 July 2026 is about GHS 1.84/kWh for the first 300 kWh and GHS 2.24/kWh above that. This is the Q2 rate plus 3.49%, before levies and VAT, so it is a derived figure, not a published one.
- **[F]** Power outages occurred in 2024, 2025 and 2026, attributed to overloaded transformers, weak distribution cables and generation shocks (ENE-03 to ENE-06).

**Implication:** Electricity is both a **cost risk** for energy-intensive businesses (laundry, cold storage, milling) and a **demand driver** for backup-power businesses (solar). This tension is a key reason solar installation ranks highly.

## 8. Business structure and the informal economy

- **[F]** Ghana had about 1.87 million business establishments in 2024. Micro-enterprises (1–5 persons) made up 90.4% of them, and 92.3% were informal (MAC-20).
- **[F]** Only about 37% of enterprises use digital payments (ENE-40), although Ghana has 26.4 million active mobile money accounts (MAC-04).
- **[F]** The World Bank's B-READY 2025 scores Ghana 69 on regulatory framework but only 50 on public services and 52 on operational efficiency (MAC-21).

**Implication:** A formally registered, digitally organised small business is **in the top decile of its competitors on professionalism**. That is a real differentiation lever in B2B services (delivery for online sellers, installation, laundry contracts). Regulation on paper is adequate, but **execution friction** (permits, utility connections, inspections) should be budgeted in time and contingency.
